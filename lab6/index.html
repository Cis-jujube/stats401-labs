How to Read the Charts

Larger country rectangles represent higher GDP. Bold group headings name continents; smaller group headings name areas. Hover over a country, tap it, or focus it with Tab to see its full name, hierarchy, GDP, and status. Small labels are shortened or hidden to fit; full names are available in the tooltips and table. On small screens, scroll each chart sideways.

Design Description

I use rectangle area to show GDP because it helps compare each country's contribution to the total. Blue means Increase, gray means Unchanged, and orange means Decrease. The same colors are used in both charts, so status can be compared without learning a second legend. Continent headings and area headings show the grouping, while country labels identify the smallest rectangles. Tooltips provide exact GDP values and the full hierarchy when a label is too short to read. Squarify aims for rectangles closer to squares, which often makes country labels easier to place. Binary splits a group into two smaller groups repeatedly. This changes the positions and shapes, even though the data are identical. Both layouts can give small countries little room, so I include a table of full names and values. I keep the chart sizes and sorting the same for comparison. Borders and heading spaces make the hierarchy clearer, but they also use some of the available area.
